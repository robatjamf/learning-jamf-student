Crown's Crowns

A curated guide to the world's most legendary crowns — who made them, who wears them, and who owns them today.

✦

Every crown tells a story of power, ceremony, and history. Here are six of the most significant crowns ever made.

The World's Great Crowns

Ceremonial Crown

The Imperial State Crown

🇬🇧 The British Royal Family — King Charles III

One of the most jewel-encrusted crowns in existence, set with 2,868 diamonds, 17 sapphires, 11 emeralds, and 269 pearls. It is worn by the British monarch at the State Opening of Parliament each year — not at the coronation itself.

Made 1937
Location Tower of London
Diamonds 2,868

Coronation Crown

The Crown of Saint Edward

🇬🇧 The British Royal Family — King Charles III

The most sacred crown in the British regalia, used only at the moment of coronation. Solid gold, weighing over 2 kg, it is modelled on a crown belonging to Edward the Confessor in the 11th century. King Charles III wore it in May 2023.

Made 1661
Location Tower of London
Weight 2.23 kg

Imperial Crown

The Imperial Crown of Russia

🇷🇺 The Russian Federation — Kremlin Armoury, Moscow

Created for the coronation of Catherine the Great in 1762, this crown features 4,936 diamonds, 75 pearls, and a 398-carat red spinel at its peak — one of the largest in the world. It was used for every Russian Imperial coronation until 1917.

Made 1762
Location Kremlin, Moscow
Diamonds 4,936

Ancient Crown

The Iron Crown of Lombardy

🇮🇹 The Italian State — Cathedral of Monza, Italy

One of the oldest royal insignia in existence, dating to the 4th or 5th century. The inner iron band is said to be forged from a nail of the True Cross. Used to crown Charlemagne, Frederick Barbarossa, and Napoleon Bonaparte.

Made 4th Century
Location Monza, Italy
Age ~1,600 years

Medieval Crown

The Imperial Crown of the Holy Roman Empire

🇦🇹 Republic of Austria — Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna

Known as the Crown of Charlemagne, this octagonal crown is one of the most iconic medieval artifacts in the world. Used to crown Holy Roman Emperors for over 600 years, it is now preserved in Vienna and is considered a UNESCO treasure.

Made ~962 AD
Location Vienna, Austria
Used for 600+ years

Colonial Crown

The Crown of the Andes

🇺🇸 The Metropolitan Museum of Art — New York, USA

Crafted in colonial Colombia between 1593 and 1599, this crown is set with 442 emeralds totalling over 1,500 carats — more emeralds than any other object in the world. Originally made for a statue of the Virgin Mary in Popayán, Colombia. Acquired by the Met in 2015.

Made 1593 – 1599
Location The Met, New York
Emeralds 442
