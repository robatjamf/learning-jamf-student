My Notes — VMs

"A rented room in a house"

Think of it as a rented room in a house. The room (Virtual Machine) and the house is a physical computer. In simple terms your real computer (the house) uses software to carve out a slice of its power to run a pretend computer (the rented room) that acts just like a real one.

My Notes — APIs

"You don't need to know how the kitchen works"

The restaurant analogy makes perfect sense! Client = you asking. API = the waiter (the messenger). Server = the kitchen doing the actual work. The API lets two systems talk without needing to know how the other works.

Push vs Pull — in my own words

Pull: You keep checking to see if something has changed.
Push: You get notified only when something has changed.

Day 3
Day 3

Compound Engineering

The 4-Step Loop

1
Plan
Define requirements. Surface unknowns.
2
Work
Build with AI. Stay in the plan.
3
Review
Your eyes first. Then ask Claude for gaps.
4
Compound
Distill into COMPOUND.md.
1

Context Is Everything

Quality of output = quality of context. The agent only knows what you tell it.

2

Planning > Building

80% plan, 20% execute. If you're mostly watching code get written, you skipped the hard part.

3

Share Your Mental Model

The agent starts from zero. Transfer your picture of the problem to it.

4

Recognize When to Quit

Sunk cost is a trap. Starting over with better context is a skill, not failure.

5

It's a Conversation, Not a Command

Collaboration, not delegation. Back-and-forth beats one-shot prompts.

6

Review Against Your Plan

Open it. Use it. Your judgment comes first — then ask Claude for gaps.

7

Compound Your Sessions

Distill reasoning, not just outcomes. CLAUDE.md stays lean; COMPOUND.md captures everything — decisions, tradeoffs, dead ends, and why. Every session inherits everything figured out before.
