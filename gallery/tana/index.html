The Compound Engineering Loop

The workflow for building well with AI. Planning is the work. Execution is confirmation.

Work

Compound

What should the next session inherit?

Quality Check What It Means Try Asking
What are we working with? The agent knows the project — not just the task "Look at my project and tell me what you see"
What are we building? The goal is specific enough to act on "Before you start, propose a plan"
What questions came up? Unknowns surfaced and answered "What questions do you have before we start?"
What needs your judgment? Decisions only a human can make "What could go wrong with this approach?"
Warning Signs

Stop and restart if you see any of these.

Overcomplicated

Solution keeps growing — more files, more steps, harder to follow.

Context Filling Up

Conversation is long, agent repeats itself or forgets earlier details.

Approach Keeps Changing

"Actually, let's try..." with no confident direction.

You're Confused

You can't explain what the agent is doing or why.

Gut Says "Off"

Something feels wrong, even if you can't articulate it.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions for every term from the workshop.

Days 1–2 Terms

TermWhat It Is
MDM Manage many devices from one place — push settings, install apps, enforce security rules.
Like a school principal setting rules for every classroom at once.
API A way for software to talk to other software. Send a request, get data back.
A waiter taking your order to the kitchen and bringing your food back.
REST A style of API using GET / POST / PUT / DELETE over the web.
GET = read. POST = create. PUT = update. DELETE = remove.
JSON A structured format for data readable by humans and computers. Uses curly braces and key-value pairs.
Like a form: each field has a label and a value. {"name": "Tana"}
Bearer Token A code proving you've already logged in. Get it once, use it everywhere.
A wristband at an event — show ID at the door, flash the band everywhere else.
Webhook A URL that receives data when something happens. Server tells you instead of you asking.
A doorbell — you don't check the door every 5 minutes, it rings when someone arrives.
Virtual Machine A computer running inside another computer with its own OS.
A snow globe — a little world inside your world. Reset or throw away without affecting the room.
Tart A tool for running macOS VMs on Apple Silicon.
Commands: tart clone, tart run, tart stop, tart ip
Polling vs. Pushing Two ways to get updates. Polling = you keep asking. Pushing = system tells you.
"Are we there yet?" vs. "I'll tell you when we get there."
Persona A fictional character representing a real type of user, used to understand needs and pain points.
Enterprise IT Admin, K-12 IT Coordinator, Security Analyst, Help Desk Tech.

Day 3 — AI Terms

TermWhat It Is
Agent A helper that doesn't just talk — it reads files, writes code, and runs commands.
A smart assistant who can type on your keyboard, not just give advice.
Context Everything the agent knows about what you're trying to do.
Asking a stranger to grab your jacket — they need to know where you live, what it looks like, where you left it.
Prompt What you say to the agent to tell it what you want.
"Make me food" vs. "Make me a grilled cheese with tomato on sourdough."
Context Window How much the agent can remember in one conversation. Fills up over time.
A whiteboard — eventually you run out of space and have to erase something.
Hallucination When the agent says something that sounds right but isn't. Not lying — just guessing wrong.
"Turn left at the big tree" — but there's no tree. They filled in the blank.
Compounding Distilling decisions, tradeoffs, and discoveries into a reference that future sessions build on.
A pilot's logbook — record the route, weather decisions, turbulence. Next pilot starts with all that context.
One-Shot Prompting Telling the agent everything in one go and expecting a finished result. Usually doesn't end well.
"Get me a birthday present for mom" — they'll get something, but probably not what you had in mind.
Quit / Restart Stopping and starting over when things aren't working. This is smart, not giving up.
You took a wrong turn. Faster to turn around now than keep driving and hope for the best.
Transferable Knowledge

7 Principles That Last

These apply to Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor, or whatever AI tool comes next. Tools change. These don't.

01

Context Is Everything

The quality of your output is determined by the quality of your context. The agent only knows what you tell it — your mental model, constraints, the "why" behind requirements.

02

Planning > Building

80% planning, 20% building. If you're mostly watching the agent write code, you skipped the important part. The plan IS the work. Execution is confirmation.

03

Share Your Mental Model

The agent doesn't have it automatically. You've been thinking about this. Transfer that picture — "here's how I'm thinking about this..." — before you ask it to build.

04

Recognize When to Quit

Sunk cost is a trap. Starting over with better context is faster than pushing through a bad path. Quitting isn't failure — it's the skill.

05

It's a Conversation

Not a command line. Collaboration, not delegation. The best results come from back-and-forth: you share context, agent explores, you refine, together you build understanding.

06

Review Against Your Plan

Your eyes and judgment are the primary review tool. Open the output. Use it. Click the buttons. Does it match what you had in your head when you planned it?

07

Compound Your Sessions

Distill every session into a persistent reference. Not just "what I learned" — decisions, tradeoffs, dead ends, the reasoning behind choices. Make the next session start smarter.

The meta-principle: AI agents are powerful collaborators who lack your context. Your job is to bridge that gap — through thoughtful planning, clear communication, expert review, and the discipline to distill every session so the next one inherits everything you figured out.

Quick Reference

Prompts That Work

Copy these into your next Claude Code session.

Build shared understanding "Look at my project and tell me what you see"
Explore the problem "Walk me through how this works"
Surface risks early "What could go wrong with this approach?"
Get the agent's questions "What questions do you have before we start?"
Check understanding "Explain back to me what we're trying to do"
Find gaps after building "Based on our plan, what did we miss?"
Start fresh after a wrong turn "Let's start over. Here's what I've learned..."
End the session /compound
